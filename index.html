Can I have EBO2? A 30-second check

These come straight from our screening. They're safety reasons, not a judgment about you.

We don't offer EBO2 to anyone who:

  • is pregnant or breastfeeding
  • has had an organ transplant
  • has an active bleeding condition, such as a bleeding ulcer or hemophilia, or tends to bleed easily
  • is menstruating on the day of treatment (we'll just pick another date)

Dr. Nguyen reviews these first, and may say no:

  • blood thinners or steroid medications
  • sulfa drugs, including sulfa antibiotics
  • medications that make you sensitive to sunlight
  • a heparin allergy (heparin keeps blood from clotting in the circuit) or G6PD deficiency
  • serious heart disease or emphysema
  • diabetes medication such as metformin, or a history of low blood sugar
  • veins that are hard to access (we may ask you in first to check them)

If any of these apply, you can still request a consult. Mention it and Dr. Nguyen will review it.

Step by step

What actually happens

  1. 60-minute consult

    Dr. Nguyen goes through your history, medications and recent labs, and tells you whether EBO2 is appropriate. If it isn't, she'll say so.

  2. Blood sugar check

    On treatment day we check it first. The session doesn't start below 95 mg/dL.

  3. Two IV lines

    One in each arm, placed by our clinical team.

  4. About 45 minutes on the circuit

    Blood passes through the filter, the oxygen and ozone stage and a light stage called Hemealumen, and is returned.

  5. Monitored throughout

    By a physician, a nurse and a medical assistant.

  6. Home the same day

    The lines come out, you rest briefly, and you go home.

Plan for 60 to 75 minutes in the office on treatment day.

Straight answers

Risks and limits

Some people notice bruising at the IV sites, lightheadedness, tiredness afterward or a drop in blood sugar. The light stage can temporarily flare some existing conditions, such as asthma, sinus problems or rheumatic conditions. Dr. Nguyen goes through the risks for your situation at your consult.

  • The equipment we use for EBO2 is not FDA-cleared or FDA-approved, and EBO2 is not FDA-approved to treat any disease or condition.
  • It doesn't replace care from your other doctors or change the medications they prescribe.
  • We won't tell you what it will do for you before we've met you.
Questions

What people ask before booking

How much does EBO2 cost?

The 60-minute initial consult starts at $300 ($270 for members). A single session starts at $1,697 ($1,442.45 for members), and 3 sessions start at $4,197 ($3,567.45 for members). Member prices are the same at both membership levels. EBO2 is not covered by insurance. Financing is available, and HSA/FSA cards are accepted.

Why do I pay for a consult first?

Because EBO2 isn't right for everyone. The consult is where Dr. Nguyen reviews your history, medications and labs and decides with you whether it's appropriate. Some people are told no. The consult fee is separate and doesn't count toward a session.

Does requesting a consult commit me to a session?

No. The consult is its own appointment. Sessions are only booked afterward, and only if Dr. Nguyen recommends them and you want to go ahead.

How long does it take?

About 45 minutes on the circuit, and 60 to 75 minutes for the whole appointment.

Why do I need to eat before?

EBO2 can lower blood sugar. A carbohydrate meal about an hour before, plus a blood sugar check before we start, is how we manage that.

What is EBO2, exactly? Is it the same as EBOO?

Extracorporeal blood oxygenation and ozonation. Both names describe the same general procedure: blood is circulated outside the body through a filter, exposed to oxygen and ozone, and returned. The longer explainer is here.

How does membership work?

You bank a monthly amount as credit for future services: $149 a month for Conscious Bank or $299 a month for Conscious Elite Bank, each with a 6-month commitment. For EBO2, both levels pay the same member prices shown above.

Is EBO2 part of joint injection treatment?

No. They are separate procedures. If you're here about a knee, shoulder or hip, start with joint injections.
